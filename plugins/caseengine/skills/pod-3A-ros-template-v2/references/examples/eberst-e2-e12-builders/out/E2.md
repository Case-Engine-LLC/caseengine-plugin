## Location: Stuart

**Q1: What should you do at the scene of a car accident in Stuart?**

- Get out of the lanes: if the car moves, especially on I-95, Florida's Turnpike, US 1 or the Roosevelt Bridge, a second crash is the real danger.
- Call it in: the Martin County Sheriff's Office, Stuart Police or the Highway Patrol needs to document it, and which one responds depends on the road.
- Get the report started: ask the officer for the crash report before you leave.
- Photograph everything: both cars, the road, the signage, the skid marks, and any injuries.

**Q2: How long do you have to see a doctor after a crash in Florida?**

- The window is short: two weeks from the crash, and it is the deadline that catches the most people out.
- What happens if you miss it: the no-fault benefits on your own policy are gone, and they do not come back.
- Where to go: the emergency room at Cleveland Clinic Martin North, or St. Lucie down in Port St. Lucie, both count.
- Who gets caught: seasonal residents and tourists, who are out of state by the next day and gone for the season by the next month.

**Q3: Who responds to a car accident in Stuart and across the Treasure Coast?**

- It depends on the road: Stuart Police inside the city, the Martin County Sheriff's Office in the county, and the Highway Patrol on I-95 and the Turnpike.
- Why it matters: the responding agency decides where the report lives and how fast you can get it.
- Get the number: the report number at the scene saves weeks of chasing it later.

**Q4: Why does the insurance company want a recorded statement?**

- It is not for your benefit: the adjuster is building a record they can use to reduce or deny the claim.
- What they are fishing for: an admission you were partly at fault, or that you felt fine at the scene.
- You can say no: you are not required to give one to the other driver's insurer.

**Q6: Does Florida still pay you if the crash was partly your fault?**

- Partly at fault is not disqualified: your share reduces what you recover, it does not erase it.
- There is a cutoff: past a certain share of the blame you recover nothing, so the percentage is the whole fight.
- Their first number is a position: the blame the adjuster assigns early is rarely where it ends up.

**Q7: What evidence disappears first after a crash?**

- Camera footage: traffic cameras on US 1 and Kanner Highway record over themselves, and the bridge cameras are on a short loop.
- Witnesses: seasonal residents and tourists, who are out of state by the next day and gone for the season by the next month.
- The vehicles: both cars get repaired or scrapped, and the damage is the story.

**Q8: What happens if the other driver has no insurance?**

- It is common here: this state does not require most drivers to carry injury coverage, and I-95 and the Turnpike bring in out-of-state drivers on top of that.
- Check your own policy: uninsured motorist coverage on your own car is what pays in that situation.
- Look for other policies: a household policy, a commercial policy, or an employer's can all come into play.

**Q9: How long do you have to file a car accident lawsuit in Stuart?**

- The clock is shorter than it used to be: the deadline was cut recently and a lot of people are working off the old number.
- Suing a government vehicle is different: a crash with a city or county vehicle in Stuart carries its own much earlier notice deadline.
- Miss it and it is over: the Martin County courthouse will not hear it, no matter how strong the case was.

**Q10: What is the most common mistake people make after a crash?**

- Waiting: the delay costs the medical deadline, the footage, and the witnesses all at once.
- Talking too much: to the other insurer, and on social media, both of which get used later.
- Taking the first offer: it lands before treatment is finished, which is before anyone knows the value.
