Producer Notes


Jurisdiction: Florida is a no-fault state where the type of crash and the type of injury sustained govern both the entry point into the system and the ceiling on what a claimant can recover. The Florida Motor Vehicle No-Fault Law (Fla. Stat. §627.736) routes every injured driver and passenger through their own Personal Injury Protection (PIP - $10,000 Florida Minimum) layer first, regardless of fault, and the 14-Day Rule (Fla. Stat. §627.736(1)(a)) forfeits PIP entirely if initial medical treatment is delayed past two weeks. The Emergency Medical Condition (EMC) Designation from a qualifying provider is what unlocks the full ten thousand dollar layer; without it the cap drops to twenty-five hundred dollars. To step outside no-fault and pursue Non-Economic Damages (Pain and Suffering) from the at-fault driver, the injury must clear the Tort Threshold / Serious Injury Threshold (Fla. Stat. §627.737), which requires permanent injury, significant and permanent loss of an important bodily function, permanent scarring or disfigurement, or death. HB 837, signed March 24, 2023, rewrote the recovery framework: the negligence statute of limitations under Fla. Stat. §95.11 dropped from four years to two, the Modified Comparative Negligence - 51% Bar (Fla. Stat. §768.81) replaced pure comparative fault, and admissibility of medical damages tightened. Crash mechanics drive injury patterns: rear-end collisions trigger the Rear-End Presumption of Negligence (Clampitt v. D.J. Spencer Sales) and produce whiplash and soft-tissue injuries the carrier will attack with the MIST (Minor Impact Soft Tissue) Defense; head-on and rollover crashes produce catastrophic injuries including Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) and Spinal Cord Injury; T-bone crashes produce side-impact trauma. Damages calculation flows through Maximum Medical Improvement (MMI), the multiplier method or per diem method for Pain and Suffering, and a Life Care Plan for catastrophic claims. The Eggshell Plaintiff Doctrine protects claimants whose pre-existing conditions make them uniquely vulnerable. Punitive Damages (Fla. Stat. §768.72) require a court-approved proffer and apply in cases of gross negligence or intoxication. Wrongful Death (Fla. Stat. §768.21) actions follow the same two-year SOL under §95.11(4)(d). Insurance practice is regulated by the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation (FLOIR) under the Florida Department of Financial Services (DFS); statutory bad-faith remedies live at Fla. Stat. §624.155. Investigation runs through the Florida Highway Patrol (FHP); licensing and crash records sit at the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV).

S1: Florida Crash Types and the Injuries They Produce (~13 minutes) - Clusters: Pillar 1 Accident Type drives Injury Pattern + Bridges (Catastrophic Injury)


Q1: What are the most common Florida car accident types and how do they each tend to hurt people differently? (3 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Most people think a crash is just a crash. But there is a real pattern in how each type of impact damages the human body.


What are the most common Florida car accident types and how do they each tend to hurt people differently?


[Attorney Response]


* Rear-end collisions: the most common Florida crash type, typically producing Whiplash, Soft Tissue Injury, and herniated discs from the forward-back acceleration
* Side-impact (T-bone) collisions: intersection crashes that produce side-impact trauma including head, thorax, and pelvis injuries when door intrusion happens
* Head-on collisions: the most lethal type, often producing Catastrophic Injury including Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI), Spinal Cord Injury, and Wrongful Death (Fla. Stat. §768.21)
* Rollover accidents: roof-crush and ejection mechanisms producing Spinal Cord Injury, paralysis, and Internal Organ Injury
* Multi-vehicle pileups: chain-reaction crashes on I-95 or Florida's Turnpike that complicate fault allocation under Modified Comparative Negligence (Fla. Stat. §768.81)
* Hit-and-run: triggers Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Coverage (UM/UIM) under Fla. Stat. §627.727 when reported to the Florida Highway Patrol (FHP) immediately
* Pedestrian and motorcycle accidents: vulnerable-road-user crashes that almost always exceed PIP limits and clear the §627.737 threshold


Q2: Why do rear-end collisions in Florida so reliably produce whiplash and soft-tissue injuries, and how does the Clampitt rear-end presumption strengthen the claim? (3 minutes)


[Co-Host]


The rear-end is the most common Florida crash, and it is also the one carriers fight the hardest.


Why do rear-end collisions in Florida so reliably produce whiplash and soft-tissue injuries, and how does the Clampitt rear-end presumption strengthen the claim?


[Attorney Response]


* Mechanics produce whiplash: rear-end impacts cause forward-back acceleration that strains the cervical spine and produces Whiplash and Soft Tissue Injury
* Symptoms can be delayed: stiffness and pain often surface 24 to 72 hours after the crash, which is exactly why the 14-Day Rule (Fla. Stat. §627.736(1)(a)) matters
* Clampitt presumption: under the Rear-End Presumption of Negligence (Clampitt v. D.J. Spencer Sales), the following driver carries the legal burden when the lead vehicle was legally stopped or stopping
* MIST defense pushback: carriers deploy the MIST (Minor Impact Soft Tissue) Defense to argue low-speed impacts cannot cause real injury; counter with diagnostic imaging and biomechanical expert testimony
* Diagnostic imaging: MRI and CT scans document soft-tissue and disc pathology that a vehicle-damage photo cannot capture
* Continuity of care: treat continuously through Maximum Medical Improvement (MMI) so the carrier cannot argue gaps mean the injury resolved


Q3: What makes head-on collisions and rollover accidents so often produce catastrophic injuries, and how does that change the compensation picture? (3 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Head-on and rollover crashes are different categories. The damages numbers are different too.


What makes head-on collisions and rollover accidents so often produce catastrophic injuries, and how does that change the compensation picture?


[Attorney Response]


* Combined-force impact: head-on crashes combine the kinetic energy of both vehicles, often producing Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI), Spinal Cord Injury, multiple Fractures (Bone), and Internal Organ Injury
* Rollover mechanisms: roof crush, ejection, and side-rollover events produce paralysis, Catastrophic Injury, and frequent fatality
* Threshold cleared automatically: these injuries clear the Tort Threshold (Fla. Stat. §627.737) threshold without question, unlocking Non-Economic Damages (Pain and Suffering)
* Future damages dominate: Future Medical Costs, Loss of Earning Capacity, Loss of Consortium, and Hedonic Damages typically exceed any past damages
* Life care plan: a Life Care Plan prepared by a certified life care planner becomes the centerpiece of the demand
* Wrongful death: when a crash is fatal, the Wrongful Death (Fla. Stat. §768.21) action runs on the same two-year SOL under Fla. Stat. §95.11
* Vehicle defect inquiry: rollover cases often include a product liability angle against the manufacturer for roof-crush or rollover-propensity defects


Q4: How do T-bone crashes at Florida intersections and side-impact collisions produce a different injury and liability profile than rear-ends? (2 minutes)


[Co-Host]


T-bone crashes are an intersection problem with a particular injury profile.


How do T-bone crashes at Florida intersections and side-impact collisions produce a different injury and liability profile than rear-ends?


[Attorney Response]


* Side-airbag protection is limited: door intrusion delivers force directly to the head, thorax, and pelvis with less crumple-zone buffer than frontal impacts
* Injury pattern: side-impact injuries include skull fractures, Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI), rib fractures, pelvic fractures, and Internal Organ Injury
* Liability is contested: intersection crashes turn on right-of-way evidence including traffic signal timing, traffic camera footage from the Florida Department of Transportation, and witness statements
* Crash report weight: the Florida Traffic Crash Report (HSMV-90010) from the Florida Highway Patrol (FHP) or local agency carries substantial pre-litigation weight even though the report itself is privileged at trial
* Comparative fault risk: intersection cases are where the 51% Bar (Fla. Stat. §768.81) most often shows up; one disputed left turn can flip the recovery
* Reconstruction expert: an accident reconstruction expert quantifies closing speed, point of impact, and right-of-way claims


Q5: How do multi-vehicle pileups and hit-and-run scenarios complicate Florida injury claims? (2 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Multi-vehicle pileups and hit-and-runs do not behave like ordinary cases.


How do multi-vehicle pileups and hit-and-run scenarios complicate Florida injury claims?


[Attorney Response]


* Initial-impact identification: in chain-reaction pileups on I-95 or Florida's Turnpike, identifying the initial collision drives liability for every downstream impact
* Joint and several liability: under post-HB 837 §768.81, fault is apportioned among all at-fault drivers, and the claimant's recovery depends on each defendant's share
* Multiple BIL policies: each at-fault driver's Bodily Injury Liability policy may be reachable; counsel must coordinate demands
* Accident reconstruction is mandatory: complex pileups always require a reconstruction expert
* Hit-and-run UM trigger: report any hit-and-run to the Florida Highway Patrol (FHP) or local police immediately; the report is required to trigger Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist Coverage (UM/UIM)
* Dashcam and surveillance: preserve dashcam footage from the claimant's vehicle and request commercial-truck dashcam and surrounding-business surveillance video before retention windows expire

Q6: What is whiplash really under Florida law, and why do carriers attack it under the MIST defense? (3 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Whiplash is the most common Florida car accident injury and the one carriers contest hardest.


What is whiplash really under Florida law, and why do carriers attack it under the MIST defense?


[Attorney Response]


* Cervical strain mechanism: Whiplash is a soft-tissue cervical strain caused by rapid acceleration-deceleration forces
* Real but invisible on x-ray: standard x-rays often miss soft-tissue pathology; MRI and clinical exam are the diagnostic anchors
* Diagnostic timing: symptoms can take 24-72 hours to fully present, which intersects with the 14-Day Rule (Fla. Stat. §627.736(1)(a))
* MIST defense: the MIST (Minor Impact Soft Tissue) Defense argues low vehicle damage means low injury; carriers use Colossus-style software to assign a low number
* Counter with imaging and continuity: comprehensive imaging plus continuous treatment through MMI defeats the MIST narrative
* Threshold check: persistent whiplash producing permanent loss of cervical range of motion can clear the §627.737 threshold and unlock non-economic damages


Q7: How do traumatic brain injuries and concussions get diagnosed and valued in a Florida case, and how does the Glasgow Coma Scale factor in? (3 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Brain injuries are the category where the difference between getting the right doctor and the wrong one is hundreds of thousands of dollars.


How do traumatic brain injuries and concussions get diagnosed and valued in a Florida case, and how does the Glasgow Coma Scale factor in?


[Attorney Response]


* Severity classification: Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) is classified mild, moderate, or severe using the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) at the ER
* Mild TBI is real: even a "mild" Concussion can produce post-concussion syndrome with cognitive deficits lasting months
* Neuropsychological testing: retain a neuropsychologist to document deficits in memory, concentration, executive function, and processing speed
* Future damages: project lifetime costs of neurological rehabilitation, occupational therapy, and any long-term care through a Life Care Plan
* Threshold guaranteed: a documented TBI almost always clears §627.737, unlocking Non-Economic Damages (Pain and Suffering)
* Mental anguish overlay: Mental Anguish (PTSD) from the crash plus the brain injury compounds non-economic damages
* Loss of consortium: Loss of Consortium for the spouse becomes a meaningful component when the TBI affects the marital relationship


Q8: How does a spinal cord injury or herniated disc get treated in Florida law and what does paralysis do to the compensation picture? (2 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Spinal injuries are where lifetime damages enter the calculation.


How does a spinal cord injury or herniated disc get treated in Florida law and what does paralysis do to the compensation picture?


[Attorney Response]


* Classification: Spinal Cord Injury is classified complete or incomplete; complete injuries produce paraplegia or quadriplegia
* Permanence is automatic: complete spinal cord injuries clear the §627.737 threshold without question
* Herniated disc: a Herniated Disc is sub-catastrophic but still claimable; MRI is the diagnostic anchor and surgery may be necessary
* Lifetime care costs: calculate decades of Future Medical Costs including in-home nursing, physical therapy, and mobility equipment
* Vocational expert: retain a Vocational Rehabilitation Expert to project Loss of Earning Capacity for the claimant's expected working life
* Life care plan anchor: the Life Care Plan drives the demand value
* MMI gate: wait until Maximum Medical Improvement (MMI) before assigning settlement value


Q9: How do internal organ injuries, fractures, and burns affect Florida compensation, and which clear §627.737? (2 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Internal injuries and fractures are sometimes overlooked in claim valuation.


How do internal organ injuries, fractures, and burns affect Florida compensation, and which clear §627.737?


[Attorney Response]


* Internal organ injury: Internal Organ Injury from blunt-force trauma often requires emergency surgery and ICU admission, and clears §627.737 readily
* Fractures: Fractures (Bone) including compound and comminuted fractures with hardware implants typically clear the threshold
* Burns and scarring: burn injuries and Disfigurement / Scarring satisfy the permanent-scarring prong of §627.737 directly
* Documentation: medical photography, surgical records, and dermatological evaluations anchor the demand
* Reconstructive cost: project the cost of reconstructive surgeries, skin grafts, and long-term wound care
* Hedonic damages: Hedonic Damages for loss of enjoyment of life are available alongside pain and suffering


Q10: How do PTSD, mental anguish, and emotional distress get valued in Florida injury cases, and when do they clear §627.737? (2 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Psychological injury is real injury under Florida law, but the threshold rules are different.


How do PTSD, mental anguish, and emotional distress get valued in Florida injury cases, and when do they clear §627.737?


[Attorney Response]


* Formal diagnosis required: Mental Anguish (PTSD) requires a formal diagnosis from a licensed mental health professional, typically using DSM-5 criteria
* Symptom documentation: flashbacks, avoidance behaviors, hypervigilance, sleep disturbance, and intrusive thoughts must be documented in therapy records
* Causal link: the mental health expert must tie the condition directly to the crash within a reasonable degree of medical probability
* Threshold gate: psychological injuries can satisfy §627.737 when they constitute permanent loss of an important bodily function (cognitive, emotional regulation), but pure-standalone emotional distress without physical injury is a harder path
* Pairing with physical injury: PTSD plus a TBI or chronic pain condition compounds non-economic damages effectively
* Future treatment: project ongoing therapy and psychiatric medication costs into the Life Care Plan

Q11: How does Florida's PIP layer interact with serious injury claims, and what unlocks the $10,000 cap versus the $2,500 floor? (3 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Florida's PIP layer is the entry point, but it is also a ceiling that catches a lot of people off guard.


How does Florida's PIP layer interact with serious injury claims, and what unlocks the $10,000 cap versus the $2,500 floor?


[Attorney Response]


* PIP is mandatory: under Fla. Stat. §627.736 every registered Florida vehicle carries Personal Injury Protection (PIP - $10,000 Florida Minimum) and Property Damage Liability (PDL - $10,000 Florida Minimum)
* 14-day window: the 14-Day Rule (Fla. Stat. §627.736(1)(a)) forfeits PIP entirely if initial medical treatment is delayed past two weeks
* EMC unlocks full layer: an Emergency Medical Condition (EMC) Designation from a qualifying provider unlocks the full $10,000 PIP layer; without an EMC the cap drops to $2,500
* Coverage breakdown: PIP covers eighty percent of reasonable medical expenses and sixty percent of lost wages up to the policy cap
* PIP exhaustion: once the $10K is exhausted, the claimant either turns to MedPay if elected or moves to a third-party BIL claim against the at-fault driver
* No mandatory BIL: Florida does not require Bodily Injury Liability on most drivers, which creates real recovery gaps for catastrophic injuries


Q12: When does a Florida injury cross the §627.737 serious injury threshold to unlock pain and suffering? (3 minutes)


[Co-Host]


The Tort Threshold is the gate to pain and suffering. Most Florida injury cases live or die here.


When does a Florida injury cross the §627.737 serious injury threshold to unlock pain and suffering?


[Attorney Response]


* Four prongs: the Tort Threshold (Fla. Stat. §627.737) requires permanent injury within a reasonable degree of medical probability, significant and permanent loss of an important bodily function, permanent scarring or disfigurement, or death
* Permanent injury proof: establish permanence through MMI determination and physician testimony tied to the AMA Guides
* Loss of function: quantify range-of-motion loss or cognitive deficit through clinical exam and imaging
* Scarring documented visually: medical photography over time documents the permanence of disfigurement
* Death automatic: in fatal cases the threshold is met by definition and the case proceeds under Wrongful Death (Fla. Stat. §768.21)
* Threshold met unlocks non-economic: once met, the claimant can pursue Non-Economic Damages (Pain and Suffering), Hedonic Damages, and Loss of Consortium


Q13: How are non-economic damages calculated in Florida using the multiplier method and per diem method? (2 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Once §627.737 is cleared, the question becomes how to actually put a number on pain and suffering.


How are non-economic damages calculated in Florida using the multiplier method and per diem method?


[Attorney Response]


* Multiplier method: the Multiplier Method multiplies special damages (medical bills plus lost wages) by an injury-severity factor, typically 1.5 to 5
* Per diem method: the Per Diem Method assigns a daily rate to the recovery period from crash through MMI
* Severity drives multiplier: mild whiplash sits at 1.5 to 2; catastrophic injury including TBI or paralysis sits at 4 to 5
* Document the impact: day-in-the-life videos, family testimony, and treating-physician testimony anchor the multiplier
* Carrier counter: insurers use Colossus-style software to assign a low multiplier; counter with comprehensive medical documentation
* HB 837 evidentiary impact: HB 837 tightened admissibility of medical damages, so the demand must align with the post-2023 rules


Q14: How does Florida's modified comparative negligence rule under §768.81 after HB 837 affect what an injured claimant can actually recover? (2 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Comparative fault in Florida changed dramatically with HB 837 in 2023.


How does Florida's modified comparative negligence rule under §768.81 after HB 837 affect what an injured claimant can actually recover?


[Attorney Response]


* 51% bar: under Modified Comparative Negligence - 51% Bar (Fla. Stat. §768.81), a claimant found more than fifty percent at fault recovers nothing
* Pure comparative is gone: before HB 837 took effect on March 24, 2023, Florida ran on pure comparative negligence; that rule is dead for new claims
* Proportional reduction: for claimants at 50% or less, damages are reduced by their percentage of fault
* One percentage point matters: the difference between 50% (full reduced recovery) and 51% (zero recovery) is one of the most consequential lines in Florida law
* Evidence drives allocation: photos, witnesses, the Florida Traffic Crash Report (HSMV-90010), and reconstruction testimony all push fault away from the claimant
* Counsel pushback: counsel contests inflated fault assignments the carrier or defense pushes toward 51%


Q15: How does the eggshell plaintiff doctrine protect a Florida claimant with a pre-existing condition, and how do carriers attack it? (3 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Pre-existing conditions are the carrier's favorite defense and the eggshell plaintiff doctrine is how Florida law protects against it.


How does the eggshell plaintiff doctrine protect a Florida claimant with a pre-existing condition, and how do carriers attack it?


[Attorney Response]


* Eggshell plaintiff rule: the Eggshell Plaintiff Doctrine holds the at-fault driver responsible for the full extent of injuries, even when the claimant was uniquely vulnerable due to a pre-existing condition
* Aggravation framework: Florida law treats aggravation of a pre-existing condition as a fully recoverable injury
* Carrier attack: carriers obtain the claimant's full medical history through broad records releases and argue the injury is pre-existing rather than crash-caused
* Limit the release: any Release of Medical Records / HIPAA Authorization should be limited to providers and dates tied to the crash
* Pre/post comparison: present pre-accident and post-accident medical records side-by-side through physician testimony to document the aggravation
* Causation standard: every diagnosis must satisfy the Florida Bodily Injury Causation Standard, linking the injury or aggravation to the crash within a reasonable degree of medical probability

Q16: What types of damages can a Florida claimant recover beyond pain and suffering, and how do punitive damages, loss of consortium, and wrongful death factor in? (3 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Most Florida claimants do not realize how many damage categories are actually available.


What types of damages can a Florida claimant recover beyond pain and suffering, and how do punitive damages, loss of consortium, and wrongful death factor in?


[Attorney Response]


* Economic damages: Economic Damages (Medical, Lost Wages, Property) include past and future medical bills, lost wages, Loss of Earning Capacity, property damage, and out-of-pocket expenses
* Non-economic damages: Non-Economic Damages (Pain and Suffering) including Hedonic Damages and mental anguish, gated by §627.737
* Loss of consortium: Loss of Consortium for the spouse impacted by the catastrophic injury
* Punitive damages: Punitive Damages (Fla. Stat. §768.72) require a court-approved proffer and apply in cases of gross negligence or DUI; Florida Dram Shop (Fla. Stat. §768.125) reaches alcohol service to minors or known habitual drunkards
* Wrongful death: Wrongful Death (Fla. Stat. §768.21) runs on the two-year SOL under Fla. Stat. §95.11
* Property damage: straightforward but always covered outside the no-fault system
* Subrogation resolution: any Subrogation and Health Insurance Liens must be resolved before settlement funds disburse


Q17: How is the value of a serious Florida injury actually calculated through MMI, the life care plan, and the demand letter? (2 minutes)


[Co-Host]


Most claimants think the case is worth what the medical bills add up to. That is rarely the right number.


How is the value of a serious Florida injury actually calculated through MMI, the life care plan, and the demand letter?


[Attorney Response]


* MMI gate: wait until Maximum Medical Improvement (MMI) before assigning settlement value; settling pre-MMI is one of the most expensive mistakes
* Life care plan: a Life Care Plan from a certified life care planner projects all future medical and personal care costs over the claimant's expected lifespan
* Vocational economist: a Vocational Rehabilitation Expert projects Loss of Earning Capacity when the injury prevents returning to the same occupation
* Demand letter: a Demand Letter consolidates economic damages, non-economic damages, life care plan, and vocational projection into a single demand
* §768.79 Proposal: serve a Proposal for Settlement (Fla. Stat. §768.79) to trigger fee-shifting on rejection if the verdict clears the threshold
* Bad faith leverage: Fla. Stat. §624.155 statutory bad-faith exposure exists when the carrier fails to settle within policy limits in good faith


Q18: What is the statute of limitations for a Florida injury or wrongful death lawsuit today, and how did HB 837 change it? (2 minutes)


[Co-Host]


This is the deadline that ends cases permanently.


What is the statute of limitations for a Florida injury or wrongful death lawsuit today, and how did HB 837 change it?


[Attorney Response]


* Two years for negligence: under Fla. Stat. §95.11 as amended by HB 837, negligence actions accruing on or after March 24, 2023 must be filed within two years
* Two years for wrongful death: the same two-year window applies to Wrongful Death (Fla. Stat. §768.21) actions under Fla. Stat. §95.11(4)(d)
* Hard deadline: miss the window and the Florida Civil Circuit Court System dismisses the case absent a statutory toll
* Sovereign immunity: claims against state, county, or municipal entities require written presentment to the agency and the Florida Department of Financial Services (DFS) under Fla. Stat. §768.28
* Sovereign caps: $200,000 per person and $300,000 per incident absent a claims bill from the Florida Legislature
* Punitive damages pleading gate: Punitive Damages (Fla. Stat. §768.72) require a proffer and court approval before amendment


Q19: What special considerations apply when a commercial truck, rideshare, or uninsured driver caused a Florida injury crash, and when should someone hire an attorney? (2 minutes)

Commercial and uninsured cases are different categories with different recovery paths.


What special considerations apply when a commercial truck, rideshare, or uninsured driver caused a Florida injury crash, and when should someone hire an attorney?

* When to call counsel: call counsel before giving any statement, when injuries require more than one visit, when fault is disputed, when commercial or government vehicles are involved, and before signing any release
* Florida Bar referral: The Florida Bar Lawyer Referral Service and the Florida Justice Association are available resources

Entity density target: 75%. Natural conversation with specific Florida entities placed where they fit. The co-host should not sound like they are reading a keyword list.


Topic vs location mix: 65% topic (crash types, injury types, damages calculation) + 35% location (Florida statutes, no-fault, HB 837, FLOIR, FHP, Florida Civil Circuit Court System). City-level extensions push the location share to 40%+ by adding municipal entities.


HB 837 anchor: Every city extension must retain the HB 837 framing. The 2023 rewrite is the most important compensation distinguisher.


Tort Threshold anchor: §627.737 is the gate to non-economic damages and must be named by statute at least three times across the script.


MMI anchor: Maximum Medical Improvement is the settlement-timing gate; surfaced in Q2 (continuity), Q8 (spinal), Q12 (threshold), Q17 (life care plan).


Eggshell plaintiff anchor: Q15 names the doctrine and walks the carrier's pre-existing-condition defense.
