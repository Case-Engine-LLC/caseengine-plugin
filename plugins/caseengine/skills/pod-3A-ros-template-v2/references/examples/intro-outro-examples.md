# Intro & Outro Examples

Calibration for S1's opening four sentences and closing three lines. **Every pair below is real** - `BAD` shipped or was generated, `GOOD` is what replaced it. Read these before writing; they carry more than the rules do.

## BAD -> GOOD: the intro

### The topic is never named

| BAD | GOOD |
|---|---|
| today we are talking about something a lot of people in Florida end up dealing with | today we're talking about **car accidents**, something a lot of Floridians end up dealing with |
| Today is about the person who calls you the day after a crash. *(teases; never says "adjuster")* | today we're talking about **the insurance adjuster**, and what they're really doing when they call you a day after a crash |
| Today, the deadline that quietly ends a case before it starts. *(never says "statute of limitations")* | today we're talking about **the deadline that ends a case before it starts** - line 4 carries "filing deadlines and the statute of limitations in California" |

Name it, then hook it in apposition. Teasing costs the retrieval anchor and the listener.

### Stacked stakes instead of one clause

| BAD | GOOD |
|---|---|
| ...something a lot of Floridians end up dealing with, almost always with no warning, and what you actually need to know if it happens to you. | ...something a lot of Floridians end up dealing with, |

Cyle, editing live: "Everything after 'if it happens to you' - delete."

### The stakes clause that breaks on half the library

| BAD | GOOD |
|---|---|
| "and what you actually need to know **if it happens to you**" on every episode | Varies per episode, and neutral: "and what that actually means for you" / "and what most people get wrong about it" |

"If it happens to you" is nonsense on *Hiring a Lawyer and How Fees Work* and on *Filing Deadlines*. Same bug Gabe already retired from outro line 3 ("if you were hurt").

### Dramatized scene instead of a bare conditional

| BAD | GOOD |
|---|---|
| Someone is driving and **suddenly** finds themselves in a car accident, | **If** someone was just in a wreck, |
| Someone's driving, and **out of nowhere** they're in a crash. | If someone just got a call from the other side's adjuster, |

### Two asks stacked

| BAD | GOOD |
|---|---|
| What do they need to do in those first few minutes? **And if there's a case that comes to mind, take us through it.** | If someone was just in a wreck, walk us through the first steps after a crash, and the mistakes that sink a claim. |

One ask. The story invitation was cut 2026-08-21; if a case example is wanted, the interviewer asks live.

### The ask invents a beat that is not in the topic

| BAD | GOOD |
|---|---|
| walk us through what to do first, **how a car accident claim gets built**, and the mistakes that sink one *(episode is "The First Steps After a Crash and the Mistakes That Sink a Claim" - claim-construction appears nowhere in it)* | walk us through **the first steps after a crash, and the mistakes that sink a claim** |
| lay out how hiring a car accident lawyer and **contingency** fees work *("contingency" is not in the title)* | lay out **how hiring a car accident lawyer works and how the fees work** |

The ask carries the topic. Nothing else.

### Line 2 and line 4 saying the same thing twice

| BAD | GOOD |
|---|---|
| L2: ...today we're talking about what happens when the driver who hit you has no insurance. <br> L4: explain what happens when the other driver has no insurance. | L2: ...today we're talking about **who pays when the other driver has nothing**. <br> L4: explain what happens when the other driver has no insurance. |

Forty seconds apart. Line 4 owns the full phrase; line 2 gets the short human version.

### The same credential every episode

| BAD | GOOD |
|---|---|
| "Brett, you've been handling these cases in California for twelve years." x10 episodes | E7: "you've sat across from these adjusters for more than a decade." <br> E9: "uninsured claims are a core part of your practice." <br> B1: "you've watched people lose real cases to a date on a calendar." |

Tenure is the fallback, not the answer. **Exact years unknown is fine** - "for a number of years," "for more than a decade."

### Verbless - short, but broken

| BAD | GOOD |
|---|---|
| I'm Kara. **Today, the insurance adjuster**, and what they're really doing on that first call. | I'm Kara, and **today we're talking about** the insurance adjuster, and what they're really doing when they call you a day after a crash. |
| I'm Kara, here with Brett. **Today, what an injury is actually worth.** | I'm Kara, here with Brett, and **today we're getting into** what an injury is actually worth. |

Cutting the verb to save words produces a headline. Nobody says "Today, the insurance adjuster" out loud. Gabe, on the first one: "this sounds weird as fuck."

### Survives every rule and still reads wrong

| BAD | GOOD | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| L2 "...the other side's **adjuster**" / L4 "walk us through dealing with the insurance **adjuster**" | L4: "If someone just got **that call**, walk us through dealing with the insurance adjuster." | Word repeated across adjacent lines. The ask can point back at what line 2 set up |
| I'm Kara, **and** today we're talking about the adjuster, **and** what they're really after | I'm Kara. Today we're talking about the adjuster, **and** what they're really after. | Stacked "and"s - break the sentence instead |
| That's a wrap on **this one**... See you on **the next one**. | That's a wrap. ... See you next episode. | Same construction twice in three sentences |
| That's the kind of thing you only really know after more than a decade **of it**. | That's not something you learn out of a book. | Trailing "of it" is written, not spoken |

These all pass the guardrails. They only surface on the read-aloud, which is why step 9 exists.

### Written, not spoken

| BAD | GOOD |
|---|---|
| we are / you have / 16 years | we're / you've / twelve years |

## BAD -> GOOD: the outro

| # | BAD | GOOD |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | That's the episode. Brett, thank you. *(no credit clause - a required beat)* | Brett, thank you for your time. That's the kind of thing you only really know after twelve years of it. |
| 1 | That is genuinely hard to explain simply. You made it sound easy. *(three choppy sentences with the thanks)* | That's genuinely hard to explain simply, and you made it sound easy. |
| 2 | **Subscribe so you catch the next one.** Every episode is at mvppersonalinjury.com. | That's a wrap on this one. **The MVP of Personal Injury Law**. See you on the next one. |
| 3 | If you're dealing with this right now, call (833) MVP-WINS or go to themvp.com. *(no hinge, no geo)* | And before you go, if you're in **California** and you need a lawyer, reach out to **MVP Accident Attorneys** at **(833) MVP-WINS**, or online at **themvp.com**. |
| 3 | "And remember," following "we'll see you next episode" | "And before you go," / "One more thing," - acknowledges the goodbye it follows |

**Why no subscribe:** the show is a search asset, not a channel play. A CTA stack undoes the authority S1 just spent twenty minutes building.

## EDGE CASE

- **Episode 1** takes "Welcome to" not "Welcome back to," and line 2 carries a one-time launch setup: what the show does all season, for whom, in `{{STATE}}`.
- **Multi-state firm** - `{{STATE}}` resolves to a spoken phrase, flagged at generation.
- **No sourced credential available** - fall back to tenure or specific case type, both derivable from the bar admission date and the practice mix. Never invent volume, trial record, or results.
