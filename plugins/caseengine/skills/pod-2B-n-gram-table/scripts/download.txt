Producer Notes
Texas is an at-fault (tort) state. Minimum auto liability is 30/60/25 (Transp. Code §601). Modified comparative negligence bars recovery above 51% fault (Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §33.001). Personal-injury statute of limitations is 2 years (§16.003); a government defendant triggers a 6-month notice under the Tort Claims Act (Ch. 101). Exemplary damages are capped (§41.008). Venue for a Fort Worth crash is Tarrant County District Court. Crash reports (CR-3) come from FWPD and TxDOT CRIS. Level I trauma care runs through JPS / John Peter Smith and Texas Health Harris Methodist.

S1: Understanding the Brain Injury (~6 minutes)
[Co-Host]
Before we get into claims and dollars, let us start with what a brain injury actually is and how it happens.
Q1: What is a traumatic brain injury and how does it happen in an accident? (1.2 minutes)
[Co-Host]
A caller has heard the term but does not really know what counts as a brain injury.
What is a traumatic brain injury and how does it happen in an accident?
[Attorney Response]
* Define it: a Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) is damage to the brain from a sudden blow or jolt, from a Concussion / Mild TBI (mTBI) to life-altering harm.
* Mechanism: rapid acceleration or impact injures the brain even without a skull fracture.
* Link to the crash: Causation (Proximate Cause) ties the injury to the Negligence that caused the accident.
* Not always obvious: symptoms can surface hours or days later.
Q2: What kinds of accidents most often cause traumatic brain injuries? (1.2 minutes)
[Co-Host]
Someone wonders whether their type of accident is even the kind that causes a brain injury.
What kinds of accidents most often cause traumatic brain injuries?
[Attorney Response]
* Common causes: high-speed wrecks, including I-35W / I-30 Fort Worth Highway Collision crashes, plus falls under Premises Liability (Falls).
* Force matters: rollover and high-speed collisions carry the highest risk.
* Tie to fault: each cause traces back to someone's Negligence.
* Reality: even a low-speed collision can cause a Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI).
Q3: What is the difference between a mild, moderate, and severe brain injury? (1.2 minutes)
[Co-Host]
A listener was told their injury was mild and assumes that means minor.
What is the difference between a mild, moderate, and severe brain injury?
[Attorney Response]
* Grading: doctors use the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) to grade severity from mild to severe.
* Mild is not minor: a Concussion / Mild TBI (mTBI) can still cause lasting problems.
* Severe end: Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI) and Subdural Hematoma are the most serious.
* Why it matters: severity drives both treatment and value.
Q4: How does a traumatic brain injury affect a person's daily life? (1.2 minutes)
[Co-Host]
A family notices their loved one just is not the same person after the crash.
How does a traumatic brain injury affect a person's daily life?
[Attorney Response]
* Daily impact: memory, focus, mood, and sleep can all change with a Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI).
* Lingering symptoms: Post-Concussion Syndrome (PCS) can keep symptoms going for months.
* Human cost: this is real Pain and Suffering, not just medical bills.
* Document it: track the changes day to day.
Q5: What is the biggest challenge in proving a brain injury claim? (1.2 minutes)
[Co-Host]
A caller worries that because they look fine, no one will believe they are hurt.
What is the biggest challenge in proving a brain injury claim?
[Attorney Response]
* Invisible injury: a Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) often does not show on the surface.
* Causation fight: proving Causation (Proximate Cause) that the crash caused the injury is the core battle.
* The burden is yours: you must meet the Burden of Proof (Preponderance).
* Records carry it: strong Medical Records make the invisible visible.
[Co-Host]
So that is the injury itself and why it is so hard to see; next let us talk about how you actually prove it.
S2: Proving the Injury (~6 minutes)
[Co-Host]

Q6: How do CT scans, MRIs, and advanced imaging document a brain injury? (1.2 minutes)

How do CT scans, MRIs, and advanced imaging document a brain injury?
[Attorney Response]
* Imaging tools: Neuroimaging (CT, MRI, DTI, fMRI) can reveal bleeding, swelling, and structural damage.
* Advanced scans: newer imaging can catch Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI) a basic CT can miss.
* Part of the record: scans become key Medical Records.
* Expert read: an Expert Witness explains the images to a jury.

What is neuropsychological testing and why does it matter in a TBI case?
[Attorney Response]
* What it is: Neuropsychological Testing measures memory, attention, and processing.
* Proves deficits: it quantifies impairment imaging cannot show, including Post-Concussion Syndrome (PCS).
* Objective proof: results back the injury with hard data.
* Expert-driven: an Expert Witness administers and interprets it.

What role does a neurologist play in a traumatic brain injury claim?

Q9: How does an attorney prove the brain injury was caused by the accident? (1.2 minutes)
[Co-Host]
The insurer is claiming the injury came from something else entirely.
How does an attorney prove the brain injury was caused by the accident?
[Attorney Response]

* Use the report: the Texas Peace Officer's Crash Report / TxDOT CRIS (CR-3) documents the impact.
* Build the timeline: tie symptoms to the crash date with Medical Records.
* Expert link: an Expert Witness bridges the medicine and the crash.

S3: What the Case Is Worth (~6 minutes)
[Co-Host]

Q11: How are economic damages calculated in a brain injury case? (1.2 minutes)
[Co-Host]
A caller wants to know which of their losses actually carry a dollar figure.
How are economic damages calculated in a brain injury case?
[Attorney Response]
* Hard numbers: Damages (Economic + Non-Economic) start with provable costs like bills and lost pay.
* Future costs: include Future Medical Expenses, not just what is spent to date.
* Lost income: factor in Loss of Earning Capacity.
* Document everything: Medical Records anchor the totals.
Q12: How is loss of earning capacity valued after a brain injury? (1.2 minutes)
[Co-Host]
A caller can no longer do the job they trained their whole life for.
How is loss of earning capacity valued after a brain injury?
[Attorney Response]
* Beyond lost wages: Loss of Earning Capacity measures reduced ability to earn going forward.
* Prove it: vocational and economic experts project the loss.
* Expert testimony: an Expert Witness translates the injury into lost earnings.
* Big driver: for a Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI), this can be the largest number.
Q13: How are non-economic damages like cognitive and emotional changes valued? (1.2 minutes)
[Co-Host]
Someone asks how you put a price on no longer feeling like yourself.
How are non-economic damages like cognitive and emotional changes valued?
[Attorney Response]
* No invoice: Pain and Suffering and cognitive change are part of Damages (Economic + Non-Economic) but have no receipt.
* Show the change: document how Post-Concussion Syndrome (PCS) altered daily life.
* Family harm: Loss of Consortium covers the toll on relationships.
* Argued, not tallied: these are built with testimony.

S4: Fault, Defense, and Protecting Value (~6 minutes)
[Co-Host]

Q16: How does comparative fault threaten the value of a brain injury claim? (1.2 minutes)
[Co-Host]
A caller was told they were partly to blame and fears they get nothing.
How does comparative fault threaten the value of a brain injury claim?
[Attorney Response]
* The bar: under Texas 51% Modified Comparative Negligence (Tex. CPRC §33.001) you recover only if 50% or less at fault.
* Proportional cut: your Damages (Economic + Non-Economic) drop by your share of fault.
* Contest it: the insurer will inflate your Liability, so fight the split with evidence.
* Stakes: cross 51% and recovery goes to zero.
Q17: How do insurance companies fight traumatic brain injury claims? (1.2 minutes)
[Co-Host]
The insurer keeps insisting the caller's symptoms are exaggerated.
How do insurance companies fight traumatic brain injury claims?
[Attorney Response]
* Dispute causation: they argue the Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) was not caused by the crash.
* Order an exam: they use an Independent Medical Examination (IME) to minimize the injury.
* Limit exposure: they lean on Insurance Policy Limits and low Settlement Negotiation offers.
* Answer with proof: strong records blunt these tactics.
