# Algorithmic Authorship Rules

48 rules for optimizing content for Google's passage scoring, document ranking, and AI Mode inclusion while maintaining human authenticity.

## Sentence Structure (Rules 1-7)

### 1. "If" Clauses at End
- ❌ "If you are injured, contact an attorney."
- ✅ "Contact an attorney if you are injured."

### 2. "Because" Clauses at End
- ❌ "Because of the accident, she suffered injuries."
- ✅ "She suffered injuries because of the accident."

### 3. Present Tense for Certainty
- ❌ "He will come to the school"
- ✅ "He comes to the school"

### 4. Verb-First Instructions
- ❌ "Lightly whip the cream."
- ✅ "Whip the cream lightly."

### 5. List Type Selection
- **Numbered**: "best X", "steps to", "how to"
- **Bulleted**: "types of X", "features of"

### 6. Ordinal Transitions
Use First, Second, Third for instructions:
"There are four steps. First, do X. Second, do Y."

### 7. Varied Sentence Length
Mix short, medium, and compound sentences. Break when ideas are separate or for emphasis.

## Entity & Anchor Rules (8-12)

### 8. Anchor Segments
Repeat key terms between sentences to create semantic bridges.

### 9. Lemmatized Predicates
Vary word forms: "Bitcoin selling requires..." → "To sell bitcoin..."

### 10. Named Entity Repetition
Use entity name at least twice in sequence:
"Tesla Motors manufactures electric cars. Tesla Motors offers Model S, 3, and X."

### 11. Attribute After Entity
Pattern: Entity → Entity → Attribute
"Tesla Motors is an automaker. Tesla Motors produces vehicles. The price range of Tesla Motors..."

### 12. Synonym Attributes
Use synonyms with varied word orders for attributes.

## Specificity Rules (13-16)

### 13. Statistics with Sources (CRITICAL)
- ❌ "There are many results..."
- ✅ "Research shows three results according to University of Y Study (2023)."
- ✅ "Harris County experiences 1,237 crashes annually according to TxDOT data."

### 14. Main vs. Other
"There are 6 main and 40 other reasons..."

### 15. Examples After Declarations
- ❌ "There are 40 cryptocurrencies."
- ✅ "There are 40 cryptocurrencies including Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Litecoin."

### 16. Abbreviations on First Use
"Bitcoin (BTC) is the main cryptocurrency..."

## List Consistency (17-18)

### 17. Same Part of Speech
First words match: "Ensures...", "Clears...", "Spends..." OR "Protein...", "Carbohydrate..."

### 18. Consistent Phrase Patterns
Maintain same structure across list items.

## Formatting Rules (19-25)

### 19. Complete Sentences
- ❌ "The reasons for amnesia:"
- ✅ "The reasons for amnesia are below."

### 20. Bold Usage (CRITICAL)
Bold ONLY for:
- Headings
- List labels: **Label:**
- NEVER bold keywords in paragraphs

### 21. Missing Stats Alternatives
Replace unavailable statistics with logically connected alternatives.

### 22. Remove Filler Words
- ❌ "George W. Bush was also a member..."
- ✅ "George W. Bush was a member..."

### 23. No Backward References
- ❌ "As stated before..."
- ✅ State information directly

### 24. State Directional Impact
Explain what affects what and in which direction.

### 25-30. Internal Linking
- No anchor at sentence start
- No anchor at paragraph start
- Explain connection before linking
- Match anchor to target page heading

## External Sources (31)

### 31. Inline Source Integration
- ❌ "Biotin is effective... (1). References: 1. Wisconsin University..."
- ✅ "Biotin is effective according to Dr. Nephew from Wisconsin University..."

## Forbidden Terms (32-35)

### 32. Avoid "Expert" Claims
- ❌ "expert", "expertise", "specialist"
- ✅ "experienced", "skilled", "knowledgeable", "seasoned"

### 33. Avoid "Maximum" Claims
- ❌ "maximum compensation", "maximize your recovery"
- ✅ "full compensation", "fair compensation", "the compensation you deserve"

### 34. Banned Jargon
Avoid: "legal framework", "complexities", "comprehensive", "navigating", "meticulously"

### 35. Punctuation
- ❌ em-dash (—), double-dash (--)
- ✅ commas, parentheses, semicolons

### 35a. Heading Integration (CRITICAL)
First sentence after ANY heading MUST incorporate key words from that heading.

## Human Authenticity (36-48)

### 36. Journalistic Persona
Write like a journalist, not a marketer.

### 37. Human Cadence
Mix sentence lengths. Use contractions naturally.

### 38. Forbidden AI Words (CRITICAL)
NEVER use:
- aspect, challenges, delve, dive into, elevate, embrace, empower, enhance, ensure, fostering, harness, journey, landscape, leveraging, multifaceted, navigate, nuance, pivotal, realm, robust, seamlessly, tapestry, testament, transformative, vibrant
- "for example", "for instance", "in conclusion", "in summary", "it's important to note", "when it comes to"

### 39. Human Lexicon
Use: "said", "says", "pretty", "quite", "really"
Use demonyms: "Houstonians", "Angelenos"

### 40. Concrete Specifics
Cite verifiable names, neighborhoods, agencies.

### 41. Sharp Openings
Never open with weather, time of day, or sweeping history.

### 42. Varied Paragraph Openers
Avoid: "For now," "In the end," "To be sure," "Ultimately"

### 43. End When Done
Don't summarize or predict at the end.

### 44. Quote Integration
Thread quotes mid-paragraph with follow-up analysis.

### 45. Header Variety
Use sentences or questions, not generic titles.

### 46. Section Balance
Keep ~1 in 3 articles fully headed; otherwise use prose.

### 47. Name Variety
Avoid: Alex, Emma, John, Sarah + Smith, Johnson, Chen

### 48. Factual Discipline
Use qualifiers: "appears", "likely", "according to [source]"

## Quick Checklist

Before publishing, verify:

- [ ] "If/because" clauses at sentence end
- [ ] Statistics have inline sources
- [ ] No bold in paragraph text
- [ ] No AI tell-words
- [ ] First sentence references heading
- [ ] No em-dashes
- [ ] Entities repeated 2+ times
- [ ] Lists have consistent structure
- [ ] No backward references
- [ ] Concrete specifics cited
