# Algorithmic Authorship Rules (48 Rules)

These rules optimize content for Google's passage scoring, document ranking, and AI Mode inclusion while preserving human authorship.

---

## Sentence Structure Rules (1-7)

### 1. "If" Clauses Placement
Put "if" statements in the second part of the sentence.
- Wrong: "If a becomes B, do X."
- Correct: "Do X if a becomes B."

### 2. "Because" Clauses Placement
Put "because" sentences in the second part.
- Wrong: "Because of Y, the X doesn't behave..."
- Correct: "X doesn't behave... because of Y."

### 3. Certainty in Verb Tense
Use present tense for certain statements.
- Wrong: "He will come to the school"
- Correct: "He comes to the school"

### 4. Instruction Sentences
Start instruction sentences with a verb.
- Wrong: "Lightly whip the cream."
- Correct: "Whip lightly the cream."

### 5. Numeric Lists
- Numbered lists for: "What are the best," "What are the steps," "How to do X"
- Bulleted lists for: "What are the types of X"

### 6. Preferential Ordinal Terms
Use ordinal transitions (First, Second, Third) for instructions.
- Example: "There are four steps for X. First, do X. Secondly, do Z."

### 7. Varied Sentence Length
Mix short, medium, and compound sentences for natural flow.

---

## Anchor Segments & Entity Repetition (8-12)

### 8. Anchor Segments
Repeat mutual words between sequential sentences to create connection.

### 9. Lemmatized Predicates
Use different lemmatized versions of predicates in sequential sentences.
- "Bitcoin selling requires..." → "To sell bitcoin, one should..."

### 10. Named Entity Repetition
Use the named entity at least twice in sequence.
- "Tesla Motors refers to... Tesla Motors manufactures... The models of Tesla Motors include..."

### 11. Attribute After Entity
Pattern: Entity name → Entity name → Attribute of entity

### 12. Synonym Attributes
Use synonyms of attributes with varied word orders.

---

## Specificity & Examples (13-16)

### 13. Numeric Values with Sources (CRITICAL)
Always cite sources immediately after statistics.
- Wrong: "There are many results..."
- Correct: "Research shows three results according to University of Y Study (2023)."
- Correct: "Harris County experiences 1,237 crashes annually according to TxDOT data."

### 14. Main vs. Other
Present main types before others.
- "There are 6 main and 40 other reasons..."

### 15. Examples After Declarations
Always follow declarations with examples.
- Wrong: "There are 40 cryptocurrencies."
- Correct: "There are 40 cryptocurrencies including Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Litecoin."

### 16. Abbreviations
Use abbreviation in parentheses on first mention.
- "Bitcoin (BTC) is the main cryptocurrency..."

---

## List Consistency (17-18)

### 17. Part of Speech Tag Consistency
Same POS for first words of list items.
- Verbs: "Ensures...", "Clears...", "Spends..."
- Nouns: "Protein...", "Carbohydrate...", "Vitamin B..."

### 18. Phrase Pattern Consistency
Maintain same patterns across list items.

---

## Sentence Completion & Formatting (19-25)

### 19. Complete Sentences
Never leave sentences half-finished with ":", "...", or ";"
- Wrong: "The reasons for amnesia:"
- Correct: "The reasons for amnesia are below."

### 20. Bold Usage Rules (CRITICAL)
Bold is allowed ONLY for:
- Headings: ### **Title**
- List labels: **1. Label:** or **Label:**
- Specified anchor text (bold ONCE)
- NEVER bold keywords, statutes, or SEO terms in paragraphs

### 21. Converging Missing Information
Replace unavailable stats with logically connected alternatives.

### 22. No Unnecessary Words
Remove non-contextual words.
- Wrong: "George W. Bush was also a member..."
- Correct: "George W. Bush was a member..."

### 23. No Backward References
Don't reference earlier content.
- Wrong: "As stated before..."
- Correct: State the information directly.

### 24. State Directional Impact
Explain what affects what and in which direction.

### 25-30. Internal Linking Rules
- No anchor at sentence start
- No anchor at paragraph start
- No anchor in first line
- Explain connection before linking
- Match source/target page nature
- Match anchor to heading entity

---

## External Resources (31)

### 31. Integrate External Resources
Mention sources inline, not in APA format at bottom.
- Wrong: "Biotin is effective... (1). 1. Wisconsin University..."
- Correct: "Biotin is effective according to Dr. Nephew from Wisconsin University..."

## Forbidden Terms & Phrases (32-35)

### 32. Attorney Descriptors (CRITICAL)
NEVER use: "expert", "expertise", "specialist"
USE: "experienced", "skilled", "knowledgeable", "seasoned", "qualified"

### 33. Compensation Language (CRITICAL)
NEVER use: "maximum compensation", "maximum recovery", "maximize your compensation"
USE: "full compensation", "fair compensation", "the compensation you deserve"

### 34. Banned Legal Jargon
Avoid: "legal framework", "legal complexities", "comprehensive", "navigating", "meticulously", "complex legal landscape"

### 35. Punctuation Preference
NEVER use em-dashes (—) or double-dashes (--)
USE: commas, parentheses, semicolons

### 35a. Heading Integration (CRITICAL)
First sentence after any heading MUST incorporate key words from that heading.

---

## Human Authenticity Safeguards (36-48)

### 36. Journalistic Persona
Write like a working journalist, not a marketer.

### 37. Human Cadence
Mix sentence lengths. Use contractions naturally.

### 38. Forbidden AI Tell Words (CRITICAL)
NEVER use:
- aspect, challenges, climate, community, component, delve, dive into, elevate, embrace, empower, enhance, ensure, fostering, harness, journey, landscape, leveraging, multifaceted, navigate, nuance, pivotal, realm, robust, seamlessly, tapestry, testament, transformative, vibrant
- "for example", "for instance", "in conclusion", "in summary", "it's important to note", "when it comes to"

### 39. Human Lexicon Signals
Favor: "said", "says", "pretty", "quite", "really"
Use demonyms: "Houstonians", "Angelenos"

### 40. Concrete Specifics
Cite verifiable names, neighborhoods, statutes, agencies.

### 41. Non-Formulaic Introductions
Never open with weather, time of day, or sweeping history. Lead with sharp facts.

### 42. Uneven Paragraph Openers
Avoid: "For now," "In the end," "To be sure," "Ultimately"

### 43. Messy Conclusions
End when reporting stops. Don't summarize or predict.

### 44. Quote Handling
Thread quotes mid-paragraph with follow-up analysis.

### 45. Header Variety
Use descriptive sentences or questions, not generic title case.

### 46. Section Odds
Keep roughly 1 in 3 articles fully headed; otherwise use continuous prose.

### 47. Naming Discipline
Avoid overused AI name combinations (Alex, Emma, John, Sarah + Smith, Johnson, Chen).

### 48. Factual Discipline
Double-check claims. Use qualifiers: "appears", "likely", "according to [source]"
