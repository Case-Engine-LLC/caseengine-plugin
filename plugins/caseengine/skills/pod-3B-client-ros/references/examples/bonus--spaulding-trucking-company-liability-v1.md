## S1: The Atlanta Truck Accident Liability Chain (~13 minutes)

### Q1: What is vicarious liability and why does it matter in an Atlanta truck accident? (2 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

Most people watching this assume the driver is the only one on the hook.

**What is vicarious liability, and why does it matter when an [18-wheeler]{.underline} hits someone in [Atlanta]{.underline}?**

*[Attorney Response]*

- **Vicarious liability:** the [trucking company]{.underline} is legally responsible for its driver's negligent acts when those acts happen within the scope of employment - the company pays even though the company itself didn't crash the truck
- **Respondeat superior:** the Latin doctrine behind vicarious liability - "let the master answer" - and the foundation that puts the [motor carrier]{.underline} on the hook alongside the driver
- **Scope of employment:** if the driver was on a dispatched run for the [trucking company]{.underline} at the time of the wreck, scope is established and vicarious liability attaches
- **Why it matters in Georgia:** the driver personally rarely has the assets or insurance to cover catastrophic injuries from an [18-wheeler]{.underline} crash on [I-285]{.underline} or [I-75]{.underline} - vicarious liability is what brings the carrier's federally-required insurance policy into the case
- **Direct line to the carrier in Georgia:** [O.C.G.A. § 40-1-112]{.underline}, the [Georgia Direct Action Statute]{.underline}, lets the injured person name the trucking company's insurance carrier directly as a defendant - most states don't allow this, and it changes how the case posture sits from day one

### Q2: Can a trucking company be sued in Atlanta for negligent hiring of its driver? (2 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

Beyond just being the employer, the company can be directly negligent before the truck ever hit the road.

**Can a [trucking company]{.underline} be sued for negligent hiring after an [Atlanta]{.underline} truck accident?**

*[Attorney Response]*

- **Negligent hiring:** the [motor carrier]{.underline} failed to investigate the driver's history before handing over the keys to an 80,000-pound vehicle
- **What should have been checked:** prior accidents, prior moving violations, prior [CDL]{.underline} suspensions, drug and alcohol history, and prior employer references - all required under [49 CFR Part 391]{.underline}
- **FMCSA driver qualification file:** every interstate carrier must maintain a [Driver Qualification File]{.underline} on each driver - missing or incomplete files are direct evidence of negligent hiring
- **Why this matters in Atlanta:** the [Georgia State Patrol Motor Carrier Compliance Division]{.underline} and [FMCSA]{.underline} both publish carrier safety records publicly through [SAFER]{.underline} and [CSA scores]{.underline} - if a carrier hired a driver with a documented red-flag history, the carrier owns that hiring decision in front of a [Fulton County]{.underline} jury
- **Pattern evidence:** when negligent hiring is in the case, prior similar incidents involving the same carrier become admissible to show a pattern of corporate disregard

### Q3: What role does FMCSA play in determining a trucking company's liability in Georgia? (2 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

There's an entire federal regulatory framework sitting on top of every commercial truck driving through [Atlanta]{.underline}.

**What role does the [FMCSA]{.underline} play in determining a [trucking company's]{.underline} liability after a wreck in [Georgia]{.underline}?**

*[Attorney Response]*

- **FMCSA framework:** the [Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration]{.underline} sets minimum safety standards for every interstate motor carrier under [49 CFR Parts 350-399]{.underline} - covers hiring, hours of service, vehicle inspection, drug testing, and recordkeeping
- **Federal Motor Carrier Safety Act:** the underlying statute that created [FMCSA]{.underline} and gives [USDOT]{.underline} authority to regulate commercial trucking on federal highways like [I-285]{.underline}, [I-75]{.underline}, and [I-20]{.underline}
- **Hours of Service rules:** [49 CFR Part 395]{.underline} caps a driver at 11 hours of driving in a 14-hour duty window with mandatory rest periods - violations are recorded in the [Electronic Logging Device]{.underline} and become smoking-gun evidence in fatigue cases
- **Operating authority:** every interstate carrier must hold a valid [USDOT number]{.underline} and [MC number]{.underline} to legally operate - operating without authority establishes a per se violation
- **Carrier baseline duty:** [FMCSA]{.underline} regulations create a federal floor that every carrier crossing [Georgia]{.underline} must meet - falling below that floor is the basis for the next concept we're going to cover

### Q4: How does negligence per se work when a trucking company violates federal regulations? (2 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

This is the doctrine that turns a federal regulatory violation into automatic proof of negligence.

**How does [negligence per se]{.underline} apply when a [trucking company]{.underline} violates federal safety regulations?**

*[Attorney Response]*

- **Negligence per se:** when a defendant violates a safety statute designed to protect the public, the violation itself is treated as negligence - the injured person doesn't have to separately prove the defendant was careless
- **FMCSA violations qualify:** because [49 CFR]{.underline} regulations exist specifically to prevent commercial truck crashes, violating them establishes negligence per se in [Georgia]{.underline} courts
- **Common per se violations:** exceeding [Hours of Service]{.underline} limits, falsifying [ELD]{.underline} records, dispatching with known mechanical defects, operating without valid [operating authority]{.underline}
- **Effect on the case:** in front of a [Fulton County State Court]{.underline} jury, negligence per se shifts the conversation from "did the carrier act reasonably" to "the carrier broke a federal safety rule and now we're talking about damages"
- **What still has to be proven:** the violation was a proximate cause of the crash and the harm is the type the regulation was designed to prevent - both usually clear in an [Atlanta]{.underline} truck wreck case

### Q5: Can a freight broker or shipper be liable for an Atlanta truck accident? (2 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

People are surprised to learn the liability chain extends past the [trucking company]{.underline} itself.

**Can a [freight broker]{.underline} or [shipper]{.underline} be liable when their load causes a truck accident in [Atlanta]{.underline}?**

*[Attorney Response]*

- **Freight broker liability:** the [broker]{.underline} who hired the [motor carrier]{.underline} can be liable for negligent carrier selection - failing to check the carrier's [CSA scores]{.underline}, [SAFER]{.underline} record, insurance status, or [operating authority]{.underline} before brokering the load
- **Shipper liability:** the [shipper]{.underline} who loaded the cargo can be liable when improper loading caused the wreck - overweight, unbalanced, or unsecured cargo under [49 CFR Part 393]{.underline} securement standards
- **Why this matters in Atlanta:** [Atlanta]{.underline} sits at the convergence of [I-285]{.underline}, [I-75]{.underline}, [I-20]{.underline}, and [I-85]{.underline} - the third-largest freight hub in the Southeast - which means freight brokers and shippers are often involved in any case where a long-haul truck crashed in metro [Atlanta]{.underline}
- **Multiple defendants:** when the chain is fully developed, the case can include the driver, the [trucking company]{.underline}, the [broker]{.underline}, the [shipper]{.underline}, and any maintenance contractor - all subject to apportionment under [O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33]{.underline}
- **Joint and several liability:** [Georgia]{.underline} apportions damages among at-fault parties by percentage of fault, but a fully-developed case can pull from multiple insurance policies layered on top of the carrier's federally-required minimum

### Q6: What happens if a trucking company destroys evidence after an Atlanta truck accident? (2 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

This is the urgency piece - because evidence preservation can decide the case before the lawsuit even gets filed.

**What happens when a [trucking company]{.underline} destroys evidence after an [Atlanta]{.underline} truck wreck?**

*[Attorney Response]*

- **Spoliation of evidence:** the legal doctrine that punishes a party for destroying or failing to preserve evidence relevant to a foreseeable lawsuit - applies to [trucking companies]{.underline} the moment they're on notice of a serious crash
- **Critical electronic records:** [ELD]{.underline} data, [Engine Control Module (ECM)]{.underline} or "black box" data, [dashcam]{.underline} footage, [GPS]{.underline} routing logs, dispatch communications - all degradable or overwritable within days or weeks of the wreck
- **Spoliation letter:** an experienced [Atlanta]{.underline} truck accident attorney sends a formal preservation letter to the [motor carrier]{.underline} within 24 to 48 hours of being retained - this puts the carrier on legal notice that destroying evidence will trigger sanctions
- **Adverse inference:** if a [Georgia]{.underline} court finds spoliation, the jury can be instructed to presume the destroyed evidence would have been unfavorable to the carrier - one of the most powerful trial weapons in the playbook
- **Why timing matters:** [trucking companies]{.underline} typically have rapid-response teams - lawyers and adjusters - on scene within hours of a major crash on [I-285]{.underline} or [I-75]{.underline}; the injured person needs counsel just as fast to preserve the evidence chain

That's the full chain - vicarious liability, negligent hiring, [FMCSA]{.underline} regulations, negligence per se, freight broker and shipper liability, and spoliation. Now let's bring it home for someone listening who was just hit by an [18-wheeler]{.underline} in metro [Atlanta]{.underline}.
