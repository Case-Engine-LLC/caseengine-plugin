## S1: At the Scene - What to Do Right Now (~12 minutes)

### Q1: What should someone do immediately after a car accident in Savannah to protect their claim? (3 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

Most people panic after a crash and have no idea what to do first.

**What should someone do immediately after a car accident in Savannah to protect their claim?**

*[Attorney Response]*

- **Safety first:** move to a safe location if possible, especially on high-speed roads
- **Call 911:** contact <u>911 Chatham County</u> immediately - the <u>Savannah Police Department</u> handles city streets while the <u>Georgia State Patrol</u> covers highways and unincorporated areas
- **Request a police report:** even for minor collisions, always request a police report - this creates official documentation that the insurance company and courts rely on
- **Document the scene:** take photos and videos of both vehicles, road conditions, traffic signs, skid marks, and any visible injuries before anything moves
- **Exchange information:** get the other driver's name, license, insurance card, license plate, and vehicle description
- **Collect witnesses:** get names and contact information from anyone who saw the accident before they leave
- **Do not admit fault:** even saying "I'm sorry" at the scene can be used against you under <u>Georgia's fault-based system</u>


### Q2: Are you legally required to report a car accident to the state of Georgia? (3 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

Most people don't realize there's a separate state reporting requirement beyond the police report.

**Are you legally required to report a car accident to the state of Georgia, and what happens if you don't file that SR-13 form?**

*[Attorney Response]*

- **SR-13 requirement:** Georgia law requires drivers to file the <u>Georgia Uniform Motor Vehicle Accident Report</u> (the <u>SR-13 form</u>) with the <u>Georgia Department of Driver Services</u> within 30 days of an accident
- **Reporting threshold:** applies to accidents involving injury, death, or property damage over $500 under the <u>Official Code of Georgia Annotated</u>
- **Separate from police report:** the <u>SR-13</u> filed with <u>Georgia DDS</u> is a completely separate document from the police report filed by the responding officer
- **License suspension risk:** failure to file the <u>SR-13</u> within the required timeframe can result in license suspension
- **How to file:** the form is available online through the <u>Georgia Department of Driver Services</u> website or at any DDS office

## S2: Georgia's Insurance System and Filing the Claim (~15 minutes)

### Q4: How does Georgia's fault-based insurance system work for car accident claims? (3 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

Most people don't know how fault actually works in Georgia.

**How does Georgia's fault-based insurance system work for car accident claims?**

*[Attorney Response]*

- **Tort/fault state:** in <u>Georgia</u>, the at-fault driver's insurance company pays damages through their liability policy
- **No PIP requirement:** unlike no-fault states, <u>Georgia</u> does not require personal injury protection (PIP) coverage
- **Filing options:** the injured party can file against the at-fault driver's liability insurance or file through their own collision coverage and let their insurer subrogate
- **Fault determination:** fault is determined based on the police report, evidence, and adjuster investigation
- **Policy limits matter:** the at-fault driver's policy has limits - if your damages exceed those limits, recovering the difference becomes more complicated
- **Regulated by:** the <u>Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner</u> oversees all insurance practices in the state under the <u>Georgia General Assembly</u>'s statutory framework


### Q5: What are Georgia's minimum auto insurance requirements and why do they matter? (3 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

Most drivers don't realize how low the minimum coverage actually is.

**What are Georgia's minimum auto insurance requirements, and why do they matter if you are the one filing a claim?**

*[Attorney Response]*

- **25/50/25 minimum:** <u>Georgia</u> requires minimum liability coverage of $25,000 per person for bodily injury, $50,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $25,000 for property damage under the <u>Official Code of Georgia Annotated</u>
- **Often insufficient:** these minimums are frequently not enough for serious accidents - a single surgery at <u>Memorial Health</u> can easily exceed the $25,000 per-person limit
- **UM/UIM coverage:** <u>Georgia</u> law requires insurers to offer uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage - the <u>Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner</u> mandates this, though drivers can decline it in writing
- **Registration link:** the <u>Georgia Department of Driver Services</u> requires proof of insurance to register a vehicle - but having a registration does not mean the driver's policy is still active


### Q6: How does Georgia's modified comparative negligence rule affect what you can recover? (3 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

This is one of those rules that can completely change the outcome of a claim.

**How does Georgia's modified comparative negligence rule affect what you can recover?**

*[Attorney Response]*

- **50 percent bar:** under <u>O.C.G.A. Section 51-12-33</u>, if you are found 50 percent or more at fault, you are completely barred from recovering any damages
- **Reduction formula:** if you are less than 50 percent at fault, your damages are reduced by your percentage of fault
- **Concrete example:** $100,000 in damages with 30 percent fault means you recover $70,000 - but at 50 percent fault, you recover nothing
- **One percentage point matters:** the difference between 49 percent and 50 percent fault is the difference between recovering and getting zero
- **Insurer tactics:** the insurance company will push hard to assign you 50 percent or more fault to avoid paying entirely
- **Jury decides:** if the case goes to trial in <u>Chatham County Superior Court</u>, the jury assigns fault percentages


### Q7: Walk through the step-by-step process of filing a car accident insurance claim in Georgia. (3 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

Someone has been in an accident, they have documented everything, they have been to the doctor - now what?

**Walk through the step-by-step process of filing a car accident insurance claim in Georgia, and who should you contact first?**

*[Attorney Response]*

- **Notify your own insurer first:** contact your own insurance company promptly - most policies require prompt notification regardless of who was at fault
- **File a third-party claim:** if the other driver was at fault, file a claim against their insurance company as well
- **What to provide:** police report number, photos, medical records, witness statements, and the <u>SR-13</u> filing confirmation
- **Demand letter:** submit a demand letter once you have reached maximum medical improvement
- **First vs third party:** if fault is unclear, start with your own insurance company while the investigation determines liability
- **Bad faith recourse:** the <u>Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner</u> and the <u>National Association of Insurance Commissioners</u> can help if the insurance company acts in bad faith


### Q8: What types of damages can someone recover in a Savannah car accident claim? (3 minutes)

*[Co-Host]*

**What types of damages can someone recover in a Savannah car accident claim?**

# Appendix: Production Reference

## Formatting Guide

Speaker tags appear as [Co-Host] and [Attorney Response] - italic, no color change.

Bold text is reserved for two uses only: mandatory phrases the attorney should say close to verbatim, and {{PLACEHOLDERS}} that get filled before recording. Do not bold for emphasis elsewhere.

Underlined text marks named entities - specific organizations, agencies, courts, statutes, and government bodies. These are the words that drive topical authority. Hit as many as naturally land.

Attorney response bullets are a complete production checklist - every point the attorney should cover. They are not a script. Attorney uses their own words and can reorder.

Co-host setup text is 1 sentence max before the bolded question. Describes the listener's situation, never explains how things work.

No post-response co-host lines. Attorney bullets end. Next question starts. Segment transitions are the only co-host text between questions.

Q headings are the literal question the co-host asks on mic. Read them close to verbatim.
